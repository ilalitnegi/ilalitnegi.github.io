Research

Queer people turn to social media and online peer communities for support, and people who are out show support to those who are still closeted. But for queer people, being seen is always tied to being safe, so I ask how safety, visibility and queer joy can work together. Queer joy, the connection, celebration and pride people find online, is a part of this, and it is as important to protect and grow as safety. My aim is a digital society that is equitable, inclusive, sustainable and resilient for LGBTQ+ and other marginalised communities, the four goals of the ESRC Digital Good Network. I work on algorithmic bias in recommendation, moderation and language models, and on systems that cannot handle gender-fluid and changing identities; on threat models and privacy-preserving design against outing, extortion and harassment; and on community-owned tools that last beyond a project. I treat any research problem that fits a good digital society for queer communities as in scope, and I am also open to other problems that matter to queer people.

I use LLMs and language technology to study public debate and peer support, GIS and participatory mapping of safe and inclusive places and of access to health care, education and work, and AR/VR for shared spaces of identity expression and support, always designed with the community. Social science and queer studies are needed alongside computing because they show how identity, power and risk work in real lives, which categories are fair, and when a tool may expose the people it wants to protect. Computing lets us study many cases and build tools; social science helps us ask the right question.

Background

My earlier research and professional work covers GIS, AR/VR and applied cryptography, including systems built for defence applications. I am now redirecting these technical skills towards marginalised communities, studying safety and inclusion for queer people, with privacy and data ethics as a core concern.

I have submitted two papers on queer life in India to journals, and both are under review. One is on transgender rights in the digital public sphere, mapping public discourse on India’s 2026 amendment. The other is a threat model and attack-chain analysis of extortion and robbery targeting gay men through geosocial dating platforms in India, looking at how attackers exploit trust cues.

The platform enables users to connect through text-based communication and video calling, with support for both Hindi and English. Designed as an inclusive digital space to encourage communication, connection, and peer interaction within institutional environments.

Inclusive Technology, Web Application, LGBTQ+, Chat, Video Calling, Hindi, English

2026 Indradhanush Spaces Queer GIS · Inclusive technology

A web-based GIS application for discovering and registering LGBTQ+-inclusive spaces. Space owners can register their inclusive spaces on the platform, while users can explore nearby spaces through an interactive map. The application uses location-based visualization to display spaces around the user's current location and identify places within a selected geographic range.

Queer GIS, Inclusive Technology, Web GIS, LGBTQ+, Location-Based Services, Interactive Mapping, Spatial Search
